Booking a tennis court: a mobile journey review

I booked a public court near me in Beckenham on my phone, noted where the journey slowed down, and prototyped how it could flow, keeping the LTA's look and structure.

Open the prototype See the key steps

Cameron Hasan, Product & UX/UI Designer · October 2026

In short

What I noticedThe results page shows free times, but they aren't visible when you open the venue, so the journey seems to lose them at the key moment.
What I didWalked the live journey on my phone, captured each step, then prototyped the five key screens within the existing design language.
The resultTwo taps from the home page to a chosen court and time, one view of availability across all courts, and every screen passing an automated WCAG 2.2 AA check.

Try the prototype

Best on a phone. Open it full screen, or use the one here.

  1. On the home page, tap Find a court today.
  2. Tap 11:30 at Kings Hall Road. The venue opens with that time already selected.
  3. Tap Book to see the review step.

Venue names, postcodes and distances come from the live Beckenham results on 24 October 2026. Court counts, prices and availability are sample data. Sign-in and payment are out of scope.

The key finding

The results page showed Kings Hall Road with 11:30 and 12:00 free, plus 9 more. On the venue page, the timetable opened on Court 1, whose only free slot was 15:00. The other times were on Courts 2 to 4, behind a small arrow.

To someone booking, it can look like the times they just saw have gone. It's the first place I'd look in the analytics for drop-off.

The key steps

Step 1

Home and menu

Works well: clear sections, and the Play Your Way cards make the options easy to understand.

Opportunities

  • Booking sits below the news, after a long scroll.
  • In the menu, it's two levels deep under Play.

What I tried

  • A "Play tennis near you" search at the top of the home page.
  • "Book a court" as the first item in the menu.
Step 2

Search

Works well: location is pre-filled, and date and time are clearly optional.

Opportunities

  • A banner and photo push the form down the screen.
  • "Find current location" comes after the search button.

What I tried

  • A shorter header, so the form starts higher.
  • Location button under the field, plus Today and Tomorrow shortcuts.
Step 3

Results

Works well: free times shown in the results, sorted by distance, with a map view.

Opportunities

  • Pale green times are low contrast and don't look tappable.
  • "Member's times also available" could be clearer.

What I tried

  • Times as clear, high-contrast buttons.
  • Tapping a time opens the venue with it already selected.
Step 4

Choosing a time and booking

Works well: prices are shown up front and the booking button is easy to reach.

Opportunities

  • One court at a time, so free slots are hard to find.
  • A second booking form doesn't show availability.

What I tried

  • One list of free times across all courts, by time of day.
  • A button that confirms the choice, e.g. "Book 11:30–12:00 · £5".

Accessibility and approach

  • WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text and controls, and 44px tap targets.
  • Selected states use a tick and text, not colour alone.
  • Screen reader announcements for results, and errors linked to fields.
  • Checked with axe-core on every screen. Next would be VoiceOver and TalkBack testing.
  • Kept the LTA's colours, type and structure. The aim was a smoother journey, not a new look.

Next steps

  • Check analytics for drop-off between results and venue.
  • Test with five or six players, including someone using assistive technology.
  • Explore clearer member times, a "notify me" option for full venues, and gate codes in the phone wallet.

Limits: one journey on one day, seen from the outside, without the LTA's data, research or technical constraints. Sign-in, payment and desktop weren't covered.
