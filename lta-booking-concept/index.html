Booking a tennis court, redesigned

I tried to book a public court near me in Beckenham on the LTA website, mapped where the journey slowed down, and prototyped a mobile-first version that keeps the LTA look but gets people on court in fewer steps.

Open the prototype See before and after

Cameron Hasan, Product & UX/UI Designer · October 2026

In short

The problemFinding a free court took a lot of scrolling and tapping. Times shown on the results page weren't visible on the venue page, so the journey looked broken at its most important point.
What I didWalked the live journey on my phone, captured each step, listed the friction points, then rebuilt the five key screens as a clickable prototype.
The resultHome to a selected court time in three taps instead of seven or more, one consistent view of availability, and every screen passing an automated WCAG 2.2 AA check.

Try the prototype

It works best on a phone. Open it full screen, or use the one here.

  1. On the home page, tap Find a court today.
  2. On the results, tap 11:30 at Kings Hall Road. You land on the venue with that time already selected.
  3. Tap the Book button to see the review step.
  4. Go back and try Sydenham Wells Park, which is fully booked, to see the "next free" state.

Venue names, postcodes and distances come from the live Beckenham results on 24 October 2026. Court counts, prices and availability are sample data. Sign-in and payment are out of scope.

The key finding

The results page told me Kings Hall Road had 11:30 and 12:00 free, plus 9 more. When I opened the venue, the timetable showed Court 1 only, and Court 1's only free slot was 15:00. The other times were on Courts 2 to 4, behind a small arrow.

To someone booking, it looks like the times they just saw have disappeared. That's the point where I'd expect people to give up, so it's what I'd check in analytics first.

Before and after, step by step

Step 1

Home page

What I found

  • The page opens with a news carousel. Booking first appears in the "Play Your Way" section after a long scroll.
  • An app download banner and the news compete for the top of the screen.

What I changed

  • Added a "Play tennis near you" search at the top, pre-filled with the person's location. One tap goes straight to results.
  • Kept the news, Play Your Way, apps and footer in the same order and colours, just below the player task.
Step 2

Menu

What I found

  • "Book a tennis court" is two levels deep: Menu, then Play, then under "Get on court".
  • The small, light-grey uppercase section headings are likely below the 4.5:1 contrast minimum.

What I changed

  • "Book a court" is the first, highlighted item, with "My bookings" straight after it.
  • Kept the existing top-level sections so nothing else moves for people who know the site.
Step 3

Search

What I found

  • The app banner and a large photo push the form down the screen.
  • The tabs are cut off ("Bar…") with no sign they scroll.
  • "Find current location" appears after the Find a court button, when it's needed before it.
  • "Looking for your court booking?" is halfway down the page.
  • Worth keeping: the location is pre-filled, and date and time are clearly marked optional.

What I changed

  • Shorter hero, so the form starts higher. Tabs are shortened so all three fit.
  • "Use my current location" sits directly under the field.
  • Typed date field replaced with Today, Tomorrow or Choose a date. Time is optional quick choices.
  • "View my bookings" moved to the top.
  • A clear error, linked to the field for screen readers, if the location is empty.
Step 4

Results

What I found

  • Showing free times in the results is a good idea, but the pale green chips are low contrast and don't look tappable.
  • "View court details" is the only clear action, and it's a small link.
  • "Member's times also available" is ambiguous: do I need to join, or sign in?
  • 24 courts are split across pages, which is slow on a phone.

What I changed

  • Kept the green search panel, Sort and filter, Map view and list layout.
  • Time slots are real buttons: dark green text on light green with a solid border, well above AA contrast.
  • Tapping a time opens the venue with that exact court and time already selected.
  • Fully booked venues show the next free time instead of a dead end.
Step 5

Venue and choosing a time

What I found

  • A large photo, an empty "About the venue" and "0 Indoor Courts" come before availability.
  • The timetable shows one court at a time. Most of it is a grey "Not available" block, and you switch courts with a small arrow.
  • Free slots are pale green with underlined text, so colour does most of the work.

What I changed

  • One list of free times across all courts, grouped into morning, afternoon and evening.
  • A swipeable day strip showing how many times are free each day, and a duration choice (30 mins, 1 hour, 1.5 hours).
  • Selected times have a border, a tick and the word "Selected", not just a colour.
  • Empty sections hidden; facilities and directions moved into expandable sections below.
Step 6

Booking

What I found

  • A sticky "Continue to Booking" button shows on every screen before anything has been chosen.
  • A second form with start, end and court dropdowns doesn't show availability, so you can pick a time that isn't free.
  • The need for an account isn't mentioned until later.

What I changed

  • One way to book. The sticky button says "Choose a time to continue" until a time is picked, then "Book 11:30–12:00 · £5.00".
  • A short review step before sign-in, so people can check and change their choice.
  • Says up front that a free account is needed, and that the time is held while they sign in.

Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)

  • All text meets 4.5:1 contrast. Large headings meet 3:1.
  • Buttons, inputs and selected states meet 3:1 against their background.
  • Tap targets are at least 44px (AA asks for 24px).
  • Selection is shown with a tick and text as well as colour.
  • Visible focus, and the sticky bar never covers the focused item (2.4.11).
  • Skip link, a proper heading structure, and a new page title for each step.
  • Result counts and changes are announced to screen readers.
  • Errors are described in text and linked to the field.
  • Works at 320px wide with no sideways scrolling, and respects reduced motion.
  • Checked with axe-core on every screen and state: no violations. Next step is a manual test with VoiceOver and TalkBack.

Design decisions

  • Kept the brand. Same colours, condensed headings, green section bars and page structure. The aim was a better journey within the existing design language, not a new look.
  • Light refresh only. Slightly softer corners and clearer component states. A bigger visual refresh would be a design system decision to make with the brand team.
  • Mobile first. Most people book on the go, so every screen was designed at phone width first.
  • Fix the journey, not just the screens. The biggest win is carrying the chosen time from results to the venue.

Suggested next improvements

Not built in the prototype, but where I'd look next:

  • Explain member times. Replace "Member's times also available" with a clear line on who can book them and how to sign in to see them.
  • Map with availability. Show the next free time on each map pin, not just the venue.
  • Book again. Put the person's usual court and time on the home page once they're signed in.
  • Notify me. Let people ask to be told if a slot at a fully booked venue frees up.
  • Invite players. Share a booking with friends from the confirmation screen, and split the cost.
  • Gate code in the wallet. Add the booking and gate PIN to Apple or Google Wallet and the calendar.
  • Useful filters. Floodlit courts, indoor courts, surface and price.
  • One app prompt. Replace the stacked app banners with one well-timed prompt, for example after a first booking.

How I'd test it

Research

  • Check the funnel in analytics: home, search, results, venue, checkout. Look first for drop-off between results and venue.
  • Task-based usability sessions with five or six players, including occasional players, parents booking for children and at least one assistive technology user.
  • Talk to the customer support team about the most common booking contacts.
  • A/B test the "tap a time in results" deep link against the current flow.

What success would look like

  • Higher booking conversionfrom search to confirmed booking
  • Less time to bookfrom landing to a confirmed court
  • Fewer drop-offsbetween results and venue
  • Fewer support contactsabout finding or changing bookings

Limits: this is based on one journey on one day, from the outside. I haven't seen the LTA's analytics, research or technical constraints, and I didn't go through sign-in or payment. Desktop layouts weren't designed.
