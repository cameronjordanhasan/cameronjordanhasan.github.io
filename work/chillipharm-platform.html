The ChilliPharm Web Platform

A brand new platform that reduced user errors by 14% and halved the price to clients.

Project overview

Project type
End-to-end website
Role
Junior UX/UI designer, with the support from my mentor, Jess Dams
Industry
Pharmaceutical SaaS
Tools
Figma, Balsamiq Cloud
Duration
2023 to 2024

Key summary

This project showcases the end-to-end redesign of the ChilliPharm platform, improving how pharmaceutical companies upload, organise, and manage clinical trial video assets. The work focused on enhancing data integrity, reducing upload mistakes, and introducing a structured organisation system to streamline workflows for both site users and reviewers. Once the new platform launched, our data showed a 14% reduction in user errors, thanks to the improved organisation and simplified data entry process. Along the way I also designed a permissions based feature that lets de-identified and raw clinical trial videos be managed within a single system, which reduced clients’ storage requirements by 50% and ultimately cut overall costs by 50%.

UX Research

Overview

This project was initially started with a desire to do several things; improve and update on the existing website, to centralise data integrity and to build a website that can also be created into an app further down the line.

Part way through, the director of sales gave us a second problem to solve. He explained to us that the company was having to charge clients who require video de-identification double the amount because we need to bill for 2 accounts instead of 1; the ‘raw’ unprocessed videos and an account for the blurred de-identified videos. With both in mind, this led to an investigation:

Research Goals

We wanted to understand the users’ requirements at the time, so that we understood what would make their journey quicker and simpler and improve their data integrity. We also wanted to understand the requirements of each client that used the de-identification service, the internal video team’s workflow, and how they used ChilliPharm in this process.

Internal Account Manager and Video Department Interviews

Our first port of call was to interview the account managers for our clients. This was a deliberate move because the account managers understand the clients’ requirements better than the clients themselves. Furthermore different clients have different requirements so we would be able to understand all their pain points rather than just those of a single client.

For the video team, I was in a great position; having worked in the ChilliPharm video department for 2 years I already had an excellent understanding of the video team’s clients and their process, but I still executed rigorous internal research to ensure I understood the workflow of the video team and the requirements for each client.

Mistakes Happen

When uploading, site users would often make mistakes with the additional information fields leading to skewed data if this mistake was missed.

Organisation

There were some organisational tools but most clients ignored them and chose to upload assets to ‘All Content’, making assets more difficult to find.

Out with the old

The current ChilliPharm libraries system looks old and outdated, clients expect a slick and clean designed UI, something that current Libraries does not offer.

3 separate workflows

The video team has several different workflows for different clients, and some workflows include 3rd party vendors. All these workflows and considerations need to be accounted for.

Cost

The director of sales would like to halve the cost of our product for those who need video services. He believes that if it’s cheaper we could get more clients on the platform.

Some things can’t change

The video team currently use a Quality Control library in order to check that the blurred videos meet the standards of the client and can be copied over. It is imperative this part of their workflow does not change.

User Interviews

For the platform, 5 people were interviewed: 2 client users (a site user and video reviewer), 2 account managers and a member of ChilliPharm’s video service team. I conducted the internal interviews face to face and the client interviews I conducted over Zoom.

For video services, 5 people were interviewed: 2 account managers, 2 members of ChilliPharm’s video service team and the director of sales. I conducted all the interviews face to face.

Research Findings

📝Information Field

The information fields the user must fill out when uploading assets is a good feature but often mistakes happen which leads to poor data integrity and a negative domino effect.

🔒Data Integrity

Data integrity is vital to the sponsor (the client paying for the clinical drug trial). If the data is incorrect then nothing can be used.

📱Poor Designs

ChilliPharm does not look good on tablet and mobile to put it bluntly. This is an issue considering all client based users who upload to ChilliPharm do so via iPad.

👀Side by Side

Reviewers would like to be able to fill out forms without the need to keep switching between individual assets.

🔍The QC Library

The QC library is non negotiable for the video team, it is an imperative part of their workflow.

💰Half the cost

By halving the cost we can sign a wider variety of clients who are not as financially strong as others and increase the company’s overall revenue.

🤝3rd party vendors

They must also be considered as they are integral to some of the video team’s workflow.

User Personas

The platform had to work for everyone in the chain, from the nurse filming the assessment to the editor blurring it.

Persona #1

Emily Rodriguez

  • 34 years old
  • Clinical Research Nurse
  • Site User
  • Maryland

Emily is a clinical research nurse working in a hospital’s clinical trial unit. She is responsible for managing patient interactions in clinical drug trials, administering trial medications, and monitoring patients for side effects. Part of her role involves creating and uploading the assessment videos to the pharmaceutical cloud platform. These videos document trial assessments, patient interactions, and medication administration techniques, which are then used by trial reviewers to ensure procedural compliance and track the overall progress of the trial.

Goals
  • Efficient and Secure Uploading: Emily needs to upload videos quickly and securely, ensuring patient privacy is protected.
  • Clear Documentation: She needs a platform that enables her to attach documentation and annotations with her video uploads to provide necessary context for reviewers.
  • Compliance: Emily is focused on compliance and aims to ensure that all videos meet regulatory standards, especially concerning patient confidentiality and trial protocols.
Pain Points
  • Limited Time: Managing multiple patients in a trial setting leaves Emily with limited time for administrative tasks, so she values a fast and intuitive upload process, currently ChilliPharm does not let users leave the upload page when uploading.
  • Video File Sizes: Clinical videos are often high-quality and large in size, leading to slow upload speeds if the platform isn’t optimised.

Persona #2

Steve Smith

  • 22 years old
  • Junior Video Editor
  • ChilliPharm Video Service User
  • London

Steve Smith has a bachelor’s degree in film and over his short career has created a wide portfolio which is mostly focused on editing. He wanted to become a writer but also recognises that it’s a hard industry to get into and he knows he needs a job in order to live. He is very good with technology and understands the ins and outs of the ChilliPharm video team workflow. He is fairly new to the video team so is still learning.

Goals
  • Because he has a lot of work to do he wants to be able to quickly download videos, follow the instructions and begin editing as quick as possible.
  • He is still new to the team so he makes great use of the QC library. He hopes that one day all his videos will pass the QC check first time.
  • To quickly and simply identify the work instructions for each set of videos to edit/blur so he can spend more time writing his films.
Frustrations
  • When using the copy function in the QC library to copy the video that has passed QC there is no search function so he has to manually find the correct library to copy the asset to.
  • He does not like the idea of uploading a video directly into the client’s assessment page because if there is no QC on the video there might be some non blurred frames which would lead to the company getting in trouble and ultimately him getting into trouble.

UX Design

Project Goal

Our research demonstrated that the business and user goals were very similar. Therefore it was not difficult to find common ground between them and identify the eventual project goals. The main goals of the new website are: Organise data in tier system of Subject > Visits > Assessments, remove additional information fields which are proven to be a centre for mistakes and update the UI.

For video services there were 2 more goals; reduce the cost of video services to the clients, keep the video services workflow as untouched as possible.

Business goals

  • Redesign the UI of ChilliPharm
  • Gain new clients with a new selling point
  • Ensure video services will be able to use the new ChilliPharm
  • Having a new website with better data integrity is a selling point to new clients

Shared goals

  • Remove information fields and reduce mistakes
  • Improve data integrity
  • Ensure the new UI is optimised for mobile and tablet

User goals

  • Fill out forms without having to click on each individual asset
  • When uploading, be able to move around to other areas in the library without the need to stay on the same page
  • Not wanting to factory reset the iPads when the storage is full

Features Roadmap

After reviewing the first draft of the MVP, we had to increase the features on there. It was echoed to me that it was imperative that video de-identification had to be in the MVP because it was a big revenue stream for the business. As the video team brings in the most amount of money for the company we want to offer video services to clients on the new platform right away. The final MVP included features such as, creation of a site and protocol in the Administration area (not client facing), the Site landing page, the Subject Visit Assessment tier system, uploading assets, creating/reviewing of forms, creating updates and completing tasks.

Information Architecture

The information architecture was fairly straightforward. The landing page of the site houses the additional Subject, Visit and Assessment pages within it. These pages would house all the features in the MVP and all features added in later dates would also be likely added to these pages. From this I was able to easily define primary and secondary navigation. Adding video services did not change the information architecture.

User Flows

I immediately identified 3 user flows:

  • Site users who are uploading assets
  • Reviewers who use forms and review assets
  • The video services workflow, shared by 4 users: the Site User, the 3rd Party vendor, the ChilliPharm Video Services user and the Sponsor reviewer user

Wireframes

Key Design Decisions

Two decisions did most of the work on this project, one for the users and one for the business.

Decision 1

A tier system instead of free text fields

Research showed that the additional information fields were the centre for mistakes, and that most clients ignored the organisational tools and uploaded everything to ‘All Content’. So we organised data in a tier system of Subject > Visits > Assessments and removed the additional information fields altogether. Every asset now has its place, and there is no free text left to get wrong.

Result: user errors down 14%

Decision 2

Permissions instead of a second account

After gathering all the information regarding user needs and flows, I realised that there was little design work needed with the exception of a few new additions to already existing features. The real solution lay not with design, but with user permissions.

2 new user permissions were added:

Sites: View Study Procedure Assets
Can view the content of assets associated with a Study Procedure
Sites: View Raw Study Procedure Assets
Can see the unprocessed assets uploaded to a Study Procedure (hidden for Sites with ‘Requires Video Services’)

We created new permissions that would restrict which assets they could see; the site user is allowed to see the ‘raw’ files (before de-identification has taken place), but the sponsor reviewers were not allowed to see the raw files but were allowed to view the de-identified files. This also meant the system had to recognise which assets were the ‘blurred’ files and which were the ‘raw’ files. We did this by making the ChilliPharm Video Service team all ‘Super Duper Users’ in our platform.

Director’s commentary

I’m not joking, they were genuinely called Super Duper Users in the system.

This meant that the system was able to recognise that the assets they copy from the QC library into the assessment are the de-identified ones and are distinct from other assets, which in turn when the user does not have the permission Sites: View Raw Study Procedure Assets enabled, restricts their view so they only see the de-identified assets the ChilliPharm Video Service team edited. So the user knew which assets were the edited/blurred assets, they had a specific symbol.

Because raw and de-identified videos could now live in the same assessment, clients only needed one trial area instead of 2, and the video team’s QC library stayed exactly as it was.

Result: clients’ storage requirements and overall costs both cut by 50%

Usability Testing

Testing the platform

I mainly worked with components and variants throughout the whole project, because of this it was very straightforward to build the prototype.

In order to keep the flow neat I have used the same mild transition throughout. Additionally I have built an upload animation to make the prototype feel more realistic.

Testing video services

The solution we created was tested internally with 3 participants all playing a different user. One user was the Site user uploading the video, the second user was the ChilliPharm Video Service user re-uploading the de-identified video and the last was the Sponsor user who was viewing the edited asset.

The overall experience was very straightforward for everyone, however one issue was highlighted:

In order to download the video the video team had to search for the asset in the manage area using the individual asset ID. The video team did express that this slowed down their process and it was annoying not being able to download on the assessment page.

What worked

  • The permissions worked, which means we managed to solve the business problem of clients needing 2 separate instances.
  • The video team still kept their QC library.

Needs improving

  • The video team could not download on the assessment page; they found it annoying needing to download in the manage area.
  • The blinded study reviewer could not use Sites, because they would be able to see the name of the hospital, subject, visit and assessment.

Ideas

  • Can the blinded study reviewer use a library to view the edited assets?
  • We need to create a download function in the assessment page.

Iterations

Feedback from testing led to two iterations that gave clients and the video team more control over their subjects and assets.

Editing a subject

In order for the clients to edit a subject we added a kebab menu to the subject card with 3 options; Edit, Deactivate and Delete. Whenever a user makes a change to a subject they must enter a valid reason why before confirming.

Downloading assets

In order for the clients and the video service team to download assets we built a download asset function. The user clicks on the kebab menu and chooses ‘Download’, they select the assets they want to download, then on the second screen they choose the version they want to download and the title as well.

Results

14%

Reduction in user errors. Once the new platform launched, our data showed the tier system and the simplified data entry had cut mistakes by 14%.

50%

Lower price for clients who need video services, because raw and de-identified videos now live in one trial area instead of 2.

Key Takeaways

Challenge

This project overall was challenging for me but extremely rewarding because it was my first major project. The support I received from my colleagues was fantastic because every aspect of the UX/UI process was explained to me clearly and they were always on hand for feedback and constructive criticism. The knowledge we gained from user interviews and wider research was key to approaching the UI.

On top of that, the director of sales challenged us to have the video services feature ready to be released with the new platform, this meant we had to think of the solution in a matter of weeks, which in itself was a big challenge because it was a major feature.

Lessons Learned

  • Ensuring that I refer back to my user research at all stages of the design process to ensure my designs are exactly what the user needs.
  • Not to rush into designs, it can be easy to rush into the designs and showcase work, but the value of slowing down, zooming out and ensuring work is ready to be presentable and following the correct UX/UI process is invaluable.
  • Before starting a project it is imperative to have constant communication with the business stakeholders in order for everyone to understand what is expected in the MVP of the product.
