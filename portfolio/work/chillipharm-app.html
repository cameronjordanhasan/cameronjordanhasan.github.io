The ChilliPharm App

An app that saved users around 20 minutes per task, letting the world’s biggest pharma companies record, upload and store their clinical trial videos in compliant, secure cloud storage.

Project overview

Project type
End-to-end app
Role
UX/UI designer
Industry
Pharmaceutical SaaS
Tools
Figma, Balsamiq, Creative Cloud
Duration
3 months, 2024

Key summary

This project presents the full UX/UI design process for a pharmaceutical SaaS mobile app that securely stores and manages clinical trial videos. It focuses on solving real workflow challenges for medical professionals by streamlining how they record, upload and review trial content on mobile devices. The solution was validated through usability testing, with users giving positive feedback and saving around 20 minutes per task on average.

UX Research

Overview

This project started when a site user got in contact with our help desk team. They explained that the storage on their iPad (an iPad ChilliPharm sends to clients to record their assessment videos) was constantly full, even after uploading the videos to ChilliPharm and deleting them from the iPad. They also found it very frustrating that they couldn’t simply use their own smartphones to record and upload assessment videos. This led to an investigation:

Research Goals

We wanted to understand the users’ issues and validate them with the clients and with Apple themselves, so that we understood what would make the users’ journey quicker and simpler.

User Interviews

Five people were interviewed: 2 client users (a site user and a video reviewer), an account manager and a help desk team member (both internal), and a member of Apple’s customer support team. I interviewed the internal team members face to face, and conducted the other interviews over Zoom. From these interviews we discovered 4 major issues and several minor ones.

Research Findings

🧭Power of simplicity

Users need simple navigation to the correct subject, visit and assessment.

⏱️Timing is key

Users are busy medical staff. They need to upload and review videos quickly so they can move on to new work.

🚆Working on the go

Reviewers have busy schedules and would like to be able to work while commuting, which an app would allow them to do.

✨Designs for everyone

Users are used to apps with a modern, clean UI.

User Personas

Two very different people had to find the app easy: the nurse recording the assessment, and the doctor reviewing it.

Persona #1

Nora Evans

  • 55 years old
  • Nurse
  • Site User
  • New York

Nora took a bachelor’s in nursing and has had several jobs in the medical industry during her career. In her current role at a big pharma company she is a nursing assistant to a doctor in clinical drug trials. She is not the best with technology, but she has an iPhone and an iPad and regularly uses them to take pictures of her family.

Goals
  • To help people with rare diseases in medical trials
  • To complete her work as quickly as possible, because she has a lot of it and often jumps from patient to patient
  • To get home on time to spend time with her grandchildren
Frustrations
  • Technology is sometimes not her friend, and she can get confused using complicated websites on computers
  • She is afraid of falling behind with work, because she has to complete it and she already doesn’t spend enough time with her grandchildren
  • Sometimes she feels overwhelmed by complicated interfaces, and she fears that if she doesn’t learn them they will hire someone younger who is more tech savvy

Persona #2

Dave Stevens

  • 32 years old
  • Doctor
  • Reviewer
  • San Diego

Dave has a PhD in medical research. He enjoys his job, but it is not what he thought he would be doing with his PhD; he would like to be more hands on with patients rather than reviewing videos for the sponsor. He did his PhD because his younger brother died young after being diagnosed with a rare disease. He still works extremely hard, but is looking to move position soon. He recently had a child and is feeling the stress of a young family and a very busy job.

Goals

  • To do all his online paperwork before the end of the working day
  • To ensure the doctors performing the assessments are doing so correctly
Frustrations
  • To review the videos he needs an internet connection, so on his commute he can’t work because he doesn’t have a stable connection
  • He feels that if he doesn’t complete all of his paperwork in the day, he is slowing down bringing the drugs to market and helping the wider population
  • Needing a desktop computer or laptop to complete his work forces him to be inside, and he would love to be more active and work at the same time

UX Design

Prioritisation and Project Goals

While the business goals were mainly focused on fixing the iOS storage issue and reducing costs for clients, I had a deeper vision of how the app could also benefit users on a usability level, and I was able to identify where the two sets of goals aligned.

Business goals

  • Fix the iPad storage issue
  • Gain new clients with a new selling point
  • Reduce costs for clients, as they will no longer need iPads
  • Having an app on the market makes the company look more professional

Shared goals

  • Meet strict pharmaceutical industry regulations for storing and sharing clinical trial data
  • A user friendly app that covers both classic ChilliPharm and the new platform
  • Record on the phones users already know how to use

User goals

  • Download assets to my phone to review while commuting
  • Review assets and complete forms while commuting
  • Never have to factory reset an iPad because the storage is full

Features Roadmap

After reviewing the first draft of the MVP, I had to reduce the features to what users needed from an MVP. The final version included the basics: signing in, access to both classic ChilliPharm and PVA, uploading assets and creating forms.

Information Architecture

The information architecture was straightforward to set up, because I followed the architecture of the existing desktop ChilliPharm. I still had to adapt it for an app, so I built a site map considering primary, secondary and tertiary navigation.

User Flows

I immediately identified 2 user flows, each needed for both classic ChilliPharm and PVA:

  • Clinical evaluators (site users) who upload assets
  • Reviewers who use forms and review assets

Both users need access to the assessment page and need to be able to view assets, but only the reviewer needs to create and complete forms.

Low Fidelity Wireframes

I designed a sign in page and a trial and site or library page used by all users. The flows and UI split when the user taps into either a ‘Site’ (PVA) or a ‘Library’ (classic ChilliPharm). From there, the user either uploads an asset to be reviewed, or reviews the asset and completes a form.

UI Design

UI Library Components

Developing the component library was straightforward because I could build on the existing component library for PVA, but I had to adapt its typography and spacing rules for mobile devices, researching the industry standards at the time (Q4 2024) and implementing them.

A strategic decision I made was to use PVA’s colour palette and component library for the classic ChilliPharm side of the app too. That kept the brand consistent throughout the app, and made life easier for the engineering team.

Usability Testing

The prototype

I set up the key pages with a navigation bar fixed on every page, to give the impression of a working app. Because I worked with components and variants, building the prototype was straightforward. I used the same mild transition throughout to keep the flow neat, and built an upload animation to make the prototype feel more realistic.

Testing

I tested the prototype with 6 participants aged 24 to 55. Each had a set of predefined tasks: record and upload a video in both libraries, complete a task, make a comment or update, download a video and view it in the asset storage section. The flow proved very simple for users, and feedback was positive overall.

The colour of each note links it back to the research: coral notes answer a major issue, yellow notes answer a minor issue, and blue notes are new findings from testing.

✅What worked

  • The app looks great and it’s very simple and easy to use. I love how both Libraries and PVA have the same designs.

  • This is going to solve a lot of storage issues, and it’s going to make the company look a lot more professional.

  • This is going to save clients a lot of money as well, because we no longer need to sell them iPads to record the assessments with.

⚠️Needs improving

  • I am not sure how third party users will use this app. I think for the time being they will still need to use the desktop version, but as we are their clients I am not too concerned.

  • The upload animation on the desktop version is great. Can we have it here as well?

  • A site user would need the ability to edit or delete a subject if they accidentally enter the name wrong.

💡Ideas

  • Can we make sure that in future iterations, third party users will be able to use the app as well?

  • Put the upload animation and card on the app too.

Iterations

Testing led to two iterations. I added the upload animation and an upload card to the app, so users can see exactly what is uploading and what has finished. I also added a menu to each subject card, so a site user who enters a subject wrongly can edit or deactivate it without leaving the site screen.

Key Takeaways

Challenge

One of the biggest challenges for me was designing one app for both sides of ChilliPharm, Libraries and PVA. On desktop they have very different UI and components, so deciding what to include in the app was a struggle; at times it felt like designing 2 separate apps in one. Using the same component library for both saved time for me and the developers, and kept the ChilliPharm brand consistent.

I also started the high fidelity designs without using components and variants, which made every change to each screen take far longer. I quickly recognised this, and spent time creating components and updating each page to use them.

Lessons Learned

  • Create components and variants as early as possible, to make designing easier from the start.
  • Make sure the most important parts of the app are covered in the MVP, and get business executives to sign off on them.
